## Abstract

This study aimed to identify risk factors for post-injury sequelae among recreational athletes and to develop a nomogram for predicting the likelihood of such outcomes.

Data from 7,527 recreational athletes in the 2019 Sports Injury Survey were analyzed. The dataset was randomly divided into training (70%) and validation (30%) sets. Sixteen variables were selected based on a literature review. Group differences were examined using t-tests and chi-square tests, followed by logistic regression analysis to identify significant risk factors. A nomogram was constructed based on these factors, and model performance was evaluated using the Area Under the Curve (AUC), the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, and calibration plots.

The results indicated that injury severity, injury frequency, gender, recovery period, exercise frequency, and pre-exercise condition were significant predictors of post-injury sequelae. The model demonstrated good predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.78 (95% CI: 0.76–0.79) in the training set and 0.80 (95% CI: 0.77–0.82) in the validation set. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test (p = 0.622) and calibration plots indicated good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes.

These findings suggest that the proposed nomogram provides a useful tool for predicting post-injury sequelae and may contribute to the development of targeted prevention strategies in recreational sports.
