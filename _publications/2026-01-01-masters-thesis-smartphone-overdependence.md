## Abstract

**Purpose:** This study examined the effects of an eight-week resistance training (RT) program on smartphone dependence (SD) and inhibitory control in young adults with smartphone overdependence (SO).

**Methods:** Twenty young adults with SO (score ≥24) were randomly assigned to an RT group (3 males, 7 females) or a control group (2 males, 8 females). The RT group performed supervised RT twice weekly for eight weeks, while the control group maintained usual activities. Outcomes were assessed at baseline, week 4, and post-intervention, with maximal (MHGS) and relative hand-grip strength (RHGS) measured pre- and post-intervention.

**Results:** Significant group × time interactions were found for SD (p<.001), inhibitory reaction time (IRT; p<.05), MHGS (p<.01), and RHGS (p<.01), with improvements observed only in the RT group. Reductions in SD were negatively correlated with increases in MHGS (τ=−.382, p<.05) and RHGS (τ=−.351, p<.05). Decreases in IRT were negatively associated with increases in MHGS (τ=−.493, p<.01) and RHGS (τ=−.556, p<.001), and positively associated with reductions in SD (τ=.375, p<.05).

**Conclusion:** Eight weeks of RT enhanced muscular strength and inhibitory control and was associated with reduced SD in young adults with SO, supporting RT as a potential non-pharmacological intervention for SO.
