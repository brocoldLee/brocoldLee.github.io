## Abstract

This study aimed to examine the mediating effect of skill level on the relationship between participation frequency and injury severity among leisure sports participants. 
Using raw data from the 2019 Sports Safety Accident Data provided by the Korea Sports Safety Foundation, a total of 857 participants engaged in high-injury-rate ball sports (basketball, soccer, baseball/softball, and foot volleyball) were analyzed. Frequency analysis, descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and mediation analysis (PROCESS macro Model 4) were conducted.
The results indicated that participation frequency was positively associated with skill level. The relationship between participation frequency and injury severity was significant in soccer and basketball. Furthermore, skill level was found to mediate the relationship between participation frequency and injury severity.
These findings suggest that skill level plays a critical role in understanding injury mechanisms in leisure sports, highlighting the importance of incorporating skill-based considerations into sports safety education.
