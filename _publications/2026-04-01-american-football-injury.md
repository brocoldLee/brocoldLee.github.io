## Abstract

**Purpose:** This study was conducted to analyze the injury status and positional injury characteristics of Korean amateur American football players.

**Methods:** An analysis was conducted on 106 players registered with the Korea American Football Association in the Seoul area who had team activity experience of at least 6 months and a history of injury. The survey items consisted of categories for basic information, lifestyle habits, injury experience, and the injury recovery and return process. Injury characteristics were analyzed by classifying positions into Line group (LG), Big Skill group (BSG), and Skill group (SG).

**Results:** As a result of the analysis of injury-related experiences, a significant difference was found in injury situations among positions (p=.007). Specifically, the LG showed a high incidence in blocking and collision, the BSG in tackling and collision, and the SG in being tackled and non-contact situations. On the other hand, injury location and injury type did not show significant differences among positions, but injuries were commonly frequent in the knee, ankle, and shoulder, and fractures accounted for the highest proportion. In the analysis of the post-injury treatment and recovery process, no significant differences were found in all variables among positions. Regardless of position, the most frequent absence period after injury was '3 weeks or more,' and surgical intervention was the highest as a means of injury treatment.

**Conclusion:** This study is significant as basic research to identify the injury status and positional characteristics of Korean amateur American football players. Considering the high injury risk and severity confirmed through the research results, efforts to establish a systematic injury management system and position-specific prevention programs suitable for the domestic situation should be accompanied in the future.
